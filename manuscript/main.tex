\begin{abstract}
Whether a place on Earth remains habitable is decided not by any single
environmental variable but by the joint state of the planet's spheres: the
atmosphere that sets temperature and greenhouse forcing; the ocean that stores
most of the added heat and, through its carbonate chemistry, governs marine life;
the surface and subsurface hydrosphere, where aridification and the accelerating
depletion of groundwater increasingly decide whether land can support people and
crops; the Sun--Earth-space environment, where stratospheric ozone modulates the
ultraviolet radiation reaching the surface; and the solid Earth, whose volcanic
forcing and weathering thermostat set the long-run boundary conditions. These
spheres are physically coupled, yet the tools used to gauge planetary
habitability treat them apart—reducing habitability to temperature alone, or, as
in the influential planetary-boundaries framework, to a set of global aggregates
assessed independently and carrying no map. Here we argue for, and demonstrate, a
different unit of analysis: habitability modelled as an inherently multi-sphere,
spatially explicit, and observation-calibrated quantity. A per-cell Composite
Hazard Score integrates warming, ocean heat and acidification, and a real gridded
surface-water hazard field into a single map, whose area-weighted, baseline-
referenced integral is the Habitable Area Fraction (HAF). The multi-sphere HAF
falls from 0.90 in the preindustrial to 0.78 today and, by 2100, to 0.73, 0.63,
0.36 and 0.21 under the four Shared Socioeconomic Pathways; under high emissions
it collapses toward zero by 2300. The emissions pathway dominates the outcome by
about an order of magnitude over all internal modelling uncertainty. Because each
sphere enters as an independent, upgradeable module, the framework turns
habitability assessment from a collection of disconnected indicators into one
integrated, mappable, and falsifiable metric.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}
Habitability is a whole-system property. A region becomes less liveable not
because one threshold is crossed in isolation but because several of Earth's
spheres shift at once and compound on the same population: warming raises heat
stress toward the limits of human physiology\citep{Sherwood2010, Lenton2023};
the ocean absorbs the great majority of the planetary energy
imbalance\citep{vonSchuckmann2020} and acidifies as it takes up
CO$_2$\citep{Doney2009, HoeghGuldberg2007}; and the surface hydrosphere dries and
its aquifers are drawn down. Groundwater depletion in particular has become a
first-order threat to habitability: satellite and well observations show many of
the world's major aquifers in persistent, accelerating
decline\citep{Rodell2018, Famiglietti2014, Jasechko2024}, coupling human water
withdrawal to climate variability in exactly the places where warming is
sharpest. Above the surface, stratospheric ozone governs the ultraviolet dose
reaching the biosphere, and ozone and climate change interact\citep{Barnes2019,
Bais2018}; beneath it, the solid Earth sets the slow boundary conditions through
volcanic forcing\citep{Robock2000} and the silicate-weathering
thermostat\citep{Walker1981}. Habitability, in short, is multi-sphere.

The quantitative tools we use to summarise it are not. Three families dominate,
and each is single-sphere by construction. The human-climate-niche and lethal-heat
literatures collapse habitability onto temperature and humidity, producing
compelling but one-dimensional maps silent on ocean state and water
supply\citep{Xu2020, Sherwood2010}. Ocean-acidification and heat-content
assessments quantify the marine sphere rigorously but apart from the land surface
where people live\citep{Doney2009, vonSchuckmann2020}. And the planetary-boundaries
framework\citep{Rockstrom2009, Steffen2015, Richardson2023}, though explicitly
multi-variable, defines each boundary—climate, freshwater, ocean acidification,
and the rest—as a \emph{single global aggregate}, evaluated independently of the
others and carrying no spatial field; even its recent freshwater
refinement\citep{WangErlandsson2022} remains a global control variable. It can
report how many boundaries humanity has transgressed; it cannot say which
\emph{places} lose habitability, nor how the transgression of several boundaries
compounds within one grid cell. The result is a field that has mastered the parts
and lacks an operational model of the whole.

We contend that closing this gap is not a matter of adding variables to an index
but of changing the unit of analysis. A habitability metric should (i) carry
variables from more than one sphere and let them compound locally; (ii) live on a
real geographic grid, so that loss of habitability has a location; and (iii) be
tied to observations through a probabilistic calibration, so its projections are
falsifiable and carry honest uncertainty. No existing operational framework meets
all three at once. Building one is the aim of this work, and we regard it as a
deliberate paradigm shift rather than an incremental extension.

We implement this programme for the industrial-era-to-2300 regime, where
instrumental records permit genuine calibration. A per-cell Composite Hazard Score
(CHS) integrates six carried climate--ocean variables—global-mean surface
temperature, atmospheric CO$_2$, sea-surface temperature, ocean heat content,
surface-ocean pH and aragonite saturation—onto a real $0.5^{\circ}$ land grid,
together with a \emph{real gridded surface-water hazard field} that supplies the
emergent spatial structure and encodes groundwater stress, aridity, irrigation
pressure, river-stage sensitivity and irreversible aquifer compaction. The
area-weighted, baseline-referenced integral of the CHS below a habitability
threshold is the Habitable Area Fraction (HAF). We first calibrate the
climate--ocean core against surface temperature and ocean heat content, validate
it out of sample, then map habitability and its loss, decompose the uncertainty,
and finally show how the same architecture extends to the Sun--Earth-space sphere
through ultraviolet radiation. Everything below is regenerated by a single seeded
pipeline from public data.

\section*{A calibrated, validated climate--ocean core}
The climate--ocean state that drives the CHS is produced by a two-layer
energy-balance emulator\citep{Geoffroy2013, Leach2021} whose two free
parameters—the equilibrium climate sensitivity (ECS) and the ocean heat-uptake
coefficient—are constrained against observations (Methods). The calibrated
posterior is physically credible and observationally sharp
(Fig.~\ref{fig:posterior}): the ECS is $3.69$\,K (5--95\%: $3.03$--$4.31$),
squarely within the assessed range\citep{Sherwood2020}. The emulator reproduces
observed warming through the calibration period and, importantly, over a decade of
withheld observations it had never seen (Fig.~\ref{fig:gmst}), beating both a
persistence and a linear-trend baseline—that is, it extrapolates rather than
merely interpolating. It simultaneously matches the observed 2005--2020
ocean-heat-content rise (Fig.~S1). Jointly fitting surface temperature \emph{and}
ocean heat is what makes the calibration well posed, and it is possible only
because the emulator carries the ocean sphere explicitly.

\section*{Habitability declines steeply, and the pathway decides how far}
The multi-sphere HAF tells a clear story (Fig.~\ref{fig:haf}). In the
preindustrial it sits at $0.90$ by construction. It has already fallen to $0.78$
by 2020—a twelve-percentage-point loss of habitable land area from observed
warming, ocean change, and the present-day water-hazard geography acting
together. Its future then fans out sharply by emissions pathway. By 2100 the HAF
is $0.73$ under strong mitigation (SSP1-2.6), $0.63$ under a middle pathway
(SSP2-4.5), $0.36$ under SSP3-7.0, and $0.21$ under high emissions (SSP5-8.5),
tracking end-of-century warming of $1.8$, $3.0$, $4.5$ and $5.4$\,K above
1850--1900. The divergence widens to 2300: under the two lower scenarios the HAF
stabilises near $0.75$ and $0.59$, whereas under SSP3-7.0 and SSP5-8.5 it collapses
to $0.01$ and $\sim\!0$—a near-total loss of land clearing the composite
climate--ocean--water threshold. Mitigation, in other words, does not merely slow
the decline; it selects between a world that keeps most of its habitable land and
one that loses almost all of it. Two features stand out: the loss is already
underway, so the metric is anchored to a present that has observably moved; and
the trajectories are convex across scenarios, because the last increments of
warming push even moderate-baseline land over the threshold.

\section*{Where habitability is lost}
Because the CHS lives on a real grid, it also answers \emph{where}
(Fig.~\ref{fig:chs}). The 2100 CHS map under SSP2-4.5 concentrates hazard in the
subtropical dry belts and continental interiors—the Sahara and Sahel, the Arabian
Peninsula, the Middle East and Central Asia, interior Australia, the
south-western United States and northern Mexico, and Patagonia—while maritime and
high-latitude margins remain comparatively habitable. This pattern is not imposed
by hand: it emerges from the real surface-water hazard field, in which groundwater
stress and aridity are already high, modulated by the calibrated land-warming
pattern. It is exactly the spatially resolved, compounded-hazard geography that a
global-aggregate boundary or a temperature-only niche cannot express.

\section*{The pathway, not the model, controls the outcome}
A calibrated, probabilistic framework lets us ask what actually governs the
answer. We decompose the spread in the 2100 HAF into three sources (Methods;
Figs.~S2--S3). The \emph{scenario} spread—the range across the four SSPs—is
$0.52$. The \emph{weighting} spread, from re-drawing the relative importance of
the six carried variables, has a 5--95\% width of only $0.072$. The
\emph{parametric} spread, from the calibrated climate posterior propagated through
the whole chain, is smaller still, $0.031$. The emissions pathway therefore
dominates by roughly an order of magnitude over the choice of variable weights and
by more over parametric uncertainty. The projected loss of habitable land is thus
a statement about human choices, not about modelling arbitrariness—a robustness
that is only demonstrable because the framework is calibrated end to end.

\section*{Extending across the spheres: ultraviolet radiation}
The framework is deliberately built so that each sphere enters as an independent,
upgradeable channel, and the natural next channel is the Sun--Earth-space
environment. The habitability-relevant variable there is the surface \emph{dose}
of biologically effective ultraviolet-B radiation—the DNA- or erythemally weighted
UV flux—which controls sunburn and skin cancer, damages phytoplankton and crops,
and is set primarily by the stratospheric ozone column together with solar
irradiance, cloud and aerosol\citep{Barnes2019, Bais2018}. It fits the present
construction with no change of architecture: surface UV-B enters as a seventh
carried variable, oriented so that more UV is more hazardous, standardised against
its preindustrial baseline, and disaggregated onto the grid through an
ozone/latitude spatial pattern (with the polar ozone holes as emergent hotspots),
exactly as surface temperature is disaggregated through the land-warming pattern.
The same logic admits, in principle, the deeper Sun--Earth-space coupling through
the geomagnetic field and cosmic-ray flux and the solid-Earth coupling through
episodic volcanic aerosol forcing\citep{Robock2000}; on the century timescale the
silicate-weathering thermostat\citep{Walker1981} is effectively constant and sets
a fixed boundary condition rather than a carried variable. Activating the UV
channel requires only a gridded ozone/UV product and its scenario projection; the
CHS, the calibration protocol, and the uncertainty decomposition carry over
unchanged.

\section*{Discussion}
The result we most want to stress is not a single number but a change of frame.
Treating habitability as a global aggregate—the planetary-boundaries logic—or as a
single sphere—the temperature-niche logic—systematically discards the two things
decision-makers need: a location, and a compounded, multi-sphere account of what
is happening there. The framework here supplies both while remaining fully
calibrated and reproducible, and it is explicitly modular: the two-layer climate
core can be upgraded to a three-layer or prognostic carbon--chemistry
core\citep{Cummins2020}, the static water-hazard field to an evolving one, and the
Sun--Earth-space sphere activated through UV—each without disturbing the
architecture. Its headline finding is that the emissions pathway, not modelling
choice, governs how much habitable land survives the century, and that under high
emissions the multi-sphere loss is near-total by 2300. Making a spatial,
calibrated, multi-sphere metric the default unit of habitability assessment is,
we argue, the paradigm shift the field now needs.

\section*{Methods}

\paragraph*{Overview.}
The framework is a single, seeded pipeline that runs the chain: real forcing and
observations $\rightarrow$ a two-layer energy-balance climate--ocean emulator
$\rightarrow$ tempered Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) calibration of the two free
climate parameters $\rightarrow$ six carried climate--ocean variables projected
under the Shared Socioeconomic Pathways $\rightarrow$ a tiered per-cell Composite
Hazard Score (CHS) with a real gridded surface-water hazard field $\rightarrow$
the Habitable Area Fraction (HAF), from 1750 to 2300\,CE on the observed
$0.5^{\circ}$ land grid ($720\times360=259{,}200$ cells; $85{,}959$ land cells;
land area fraction $0.29$; cosine-latitude area weights). Every stochastic
operation uses a fixed, logged seed, so a run is bit-reproducible.

\paragraph*{Variables carried into the metric.}
The executed CHS is evaluated over the \emph{six} carried climate--ocean variables
listed in Table~\ref{tab:vars}, together with the tier-(ii) surface-water hazard
field, whose five observational constituents are also listed. Each carried
variable is \emph{oriented} so that larger means more hazardous (surface-ocean pH
and aragonite saturation are sign-flipped), then standardised (below). Two of the
six are read from the climate core (global-mean surface temperature is the
emulator's prognostic surface anomaly; ocean heat content is diagnosed from the
top-of-atmosphere imbalance), one is prescribed from the scenario (atmospheric
CO$_2$), and three are diagnosed (sea-surface temperature by pattern scaling; pH
and aragonite saturation by a full carbonate-system solver). The broader
multi-sphere candidate list---land-surface, cryosphere, biogeochemical,
freshwater, ozone/UV and solar variables---is the framework's design space; the
executed proof-of-concept carries the six variables that the two-parameter
emulator projects self-consistently under the SSPs, and propagates the
sensitivity to this choice through a weight ensemble (below).

\paragraph*{Standardisation.}
Each carried variable is converted to an oriented $z$-score against a common
reference,
\begin{equation}
\tilde v_m(c,t) = o_m\,\frac{v_m(c,t)-\mu_m}{\sigma_m},
\label{eq:stand}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_m$ is the preindustrial (1750--1800) baseline mean and $\sigma_m$ the
scale. Because the preindustrial \emph{variance} of the monotone anthropogenic
variables is degenerate, the scale $\sigma_m$ is taken over the full analysis
period, and it is fixed from a single posterior-mean SSP2-4.5 reference run so
that the CHS is comparable across scenarios and across posterior draws (without a
common scale, a high-emission scenario would self-standardise to its own large
variance and spuriously appear more habitable than a low one).

\paragraph*{Composite Hazard Score.}
The CHS on land cell $c$ at year $t$ is the weighted sum of the oriented,
standardised fields,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CHS}(c,t) \;=\; \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_m\,\tilde v_m(c,t),
\qquad w_m = \tfrac{1}{6},
\label{eq:chs}
\end{equation}
in which every term on the right-hand side is a function of both space and time.
The metric is assembled on the observed grid by a \emph{tiered} spatial
disaggregation reflecting the level of spatial information available for each
variable. In the executed reduction each carried field factorises as
$\tilde v_m(c,t)=P_m(c)\,z_m(t)+b_m(c)$, giving three tiers:
(i)~the surface-temperature-driven variable is pattern-scaled onto a present-day
land-warming pattern $P(c)$ (polar amplification plus a land/ocean contrast,
area-normalised to global mean one), contributing $P(c)\,s_T(t)$;
(ii)~the surface-water hazard field $B(c)$ (Table~\ref{tab:vars}, Eq.~\ref{eq:whi})
enters additively per cell and is the only tier carrying emergent spatial
hotspots, which are therefore inherited from observations rather than generated by
the forward model; and
(iii)~the ocean and global-mean variables (CO$_2$, SST, OHC, pH,
$\Omega_{\mathrm{arag}}$), having no defensible land pattern, enter as a
spatially uniform modifier $U(t)$. Collecting terms,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{CHS}(c,t) = P(c)\,s_T(t) + B(c) + U(t),
\label{eq:chs_tiered}
\end{equation}
with land area-mean $s_T(t)+U(t)$. The scheme claims no spatial skill beyond the
observed tier-(ii) field; a production metric would add validated per-variable
spatial templates.

\paragraph*{Habitable Area Fraction.}
The HAF is the cosine-latitude area-weighted fraction of land below a habitability
threshold,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{HAF}(t) = \frac{1}{A_{\mathrm{tot}}}\sum_{c} A_c\,
\mathbb{I}\!\left[\mathrm{CHS}(c,t) < \tau\right],
\label{eq:haf}
\end{equation}
where $\tau$ is the 90th percentile of the pooled preindustrial (1750--1800) CHS
distribution over land, so $\mathrm{HAF}\approx0.90$ in the preindustrial by
construction and the informative signal is its \emph{change}. Because the
preindustrial drivers are $\approx0$, $\tau$ is a fixed percentile of $B$ and the
HAF is a fixed two-dimensional function of $(s_T,U)$, precomputed once as a lookup
table and queried for the entire posterior and scenario ensemble by bilinear
interpolation. The 80th--99th percentile band is reported as a threshold
sensitivity range (Fig.~S2).

\paragraph*{Surface-water hazard field (tier-ii).}
The tier-(ii) field is a \emph{real} gridded Water Hazard Index (WHI) built
entirely from observations at $0.5^{\circ}$. For each constituent we take the
percentile rank across land pixels, $S_\bullet\in[0,1]$ (the aridity rank is
inverted to $1-S_{\mathrm{AI}}$ so that drier is more hazardous), and combine the
four indicators for which normalised weights are reported as
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{WHI} = 0.413\,S_{\mathrm{nag}} + 0.247\,(1-S_{\mathrm{AI}})
 + 0.172\,S_{\mathrm{irr}} + 0.169\,S_{\mathrm{riv}},
\label{eq:whi}
\end{equation}
where $S_{\mathrm{nag}}$, $S_{\mathrm{AI}}$, $S_{\mathrm{irr}}$ and
$S_{\mathrm{riv}}$ are the ranks of net groundwater abstraction, the aridity
index, irrigated-area density and distance to rivers; the operational raster
additionally incorporates the irreversible-compaction (land-subsidence)
constituent. The WHI is resampled to the model grid (92\% land coverage; gaps
filled with the land median), standardised over land to a spread comparable to the
forced CHS rise, and used as $B(c)$. It supplies the emergent geography of
Fig.~\ref{fig:chs} and is what makes the metric multi-sphere in substance.

\paragraph*{Random-Forest characterisation of the hazard field.}
To identify which observed predictors explain the WHI---\emph{not} to weight the
CHS---a Random-Forest regressor\citep{breiman2001random} is trained on a global
$0.5^{\circ}$ predictor stack (the variables of the design space other than the
five WHI constituents, held out to avoid circularity), split 70/30 by stratified
spatial blocking in $10^{\circ}$ latitude bands, with 300 trees, maximum depth 14
and a minimum of 7 samples per split. Held-out skill is $R^2=0.12$ with the WHI
constituents excluded, versus $0.94$ when they are included (leakage $0.82$),
which is precisely why the surviving independent predictors are used only
descriptively (Fig.~S4) and the CHS uses equal weights.

\paragraph*{Climate--ocean emulator.}
Surface and deep-ocean temperature anomalies $(T,T_d)$ evolve as the two-layer
(Geoffroy) energy-balance model\citep{Geoffroy2013} standard to FaIR-class
emulators\citep{Leach2021},
\begin{align}
C_s\,\frac{\mathrm{d}T}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \Delta F(t) - \frac{F_{2\times}}{\mathrm{ECS}}\,T - \gamma\,(T-T_d),\\
C_d\,\frac{\mathrm{d}T_d}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \gamma\,(T-T_d),
\end{align}
with $C_s=7.3$, $C_d=106$\,W\,yr\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$, $F_{2\times}=3.93$\,W\,m$^{-2}$,
and the surface feedback diagnosed as $\lambda_{\mathrm{fb}}=F_{2\times}/\mathrm{ECS}$.
The forcing $\Delta F(t)$ is the IPCC~AR6 historical effective radiative
forcing\citep{Forster2021}, extended post-2019 by the SSP total-ERF
pathways\citep{Meinshausen2020, oneill2017roads}. With piecewise-constant annual
forcing the linear system is advanced by the exact matrix exponential (FaIR's
energy-balance solver when available, a deterministic two-box forward-Euler
integrator otherwise; the two agree to the reported precision). Ocean heat content
is diagnosed as the ocean's fixed share $f_{\mathrm{oc}}=0.71$ of the
time-integrated net top-of-atmosphere imbalance
$N(t)=\Delta F(t)-(F_{2\times}/\mathrm{ECS})\,T$, i.e.\
$\mathrm{OHC}(t)=f_{\mathrm{oc}}\!\int_0^t N\,\mathrm{d}t'$. Sea-surface temperature
is pattern-scaled from global warming; surface-ocean pH and aragonite saturation
are diagnosed with the full carbonate-system solver PyCO2SYS\citep{Humphreys2022}
from the prescribed surface-ocean $p\mathrm{CO}_2$ (the SSP CO$_2$ pathway) and the
emulated SST (referenced to an $18\,^{\circ}$C preindustrial mean) at fixed total
alkalinity ($2300\,\mu\mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}$). Only ECS and $\gamma$ are
calibrated; CO$_2$ and the other greenhouse gases are prescribed from the
scenario, not integrated prognostically. A three-layer or prognostic
carbon--chemistry core\citep{Cummins2020} is the natural multi-sphere upgrade.

\paragraph*{Parameters and priors.}
The calibration samples the two parameters ECS\,$\sim\mathcal{N}(3.0,0.5)$\,K (an
AR6-consistent prior\citep{Sherwood2020}) and
$\gamma\sim\mathcal{U}(0.3,1.2)$\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$.

\paragraph*{Tempered SMC calibration.}
The pipeline (i)~draws 400 particles from the two-parameter prior; (ii)~runs the
emulator and forms the joint likelihood over HadCRUT5 GMST (1850--1980;
1981 onward withheld)\citep{Morice2021} and NOAA/NCEI 0--2000\,m ocean heat content
(2005--2020, referenced to 2005--2014)\citep{Levitus2012}; (iii)~applies a tempered
SMC pass\citep{DelMoral2006} along a 12-step inverse-temperature ladder;
(iv)~resamples systematically whenever the effective sample size falls below
$N/2$; (v)~rejuvenates with Gaussian random-walk Metropolis--Hastings using a
proposal covariance matched to the particle cloud; and (vi)~returns the posterior
ensemble of parameters, trajectories and habitability projections. Simulated GMST
is referenced to the observations' 1850--1900 baseline and ocean heat to
2005--2014 before the residuals enter the likelihood, so both terms compare like
with like. The likelihood is Student-$t$ ($\nu=4$),
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta)=\prod_i
\frac{\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu+1}{2})}{\Gamma(\tfrac{\nu}{2})\sqrt{\pi\nu}\,\sigma_i}
\left(1+\frac{(y_{\mathrm{mod},i}-y_{\mathrm{obs},i})^2}{\nu\sigma_i^2}\right)^{-\frac{\nu+1}{2}},
\label{eq:like}
\end{equation}
robust to outliers, with structural GMST uncertainty $0.20$\,K and OHC uncertainty
$10$\,ZJ. The posterior is ECS\,$=3.69$\,K [$3.03,4.31$] and $\gamma=0.54$
[$0.36,0.79$]\,W\,m$^{-2}$\,K$^{-1}$.

\paragraph*{Uncertainty decomposition.}
Because the sources of HAF spread are not commensurable, we report them
separately rather than as one additive variance: the parametric-posterior width
(200 posterior draws propagated through the emulator, CHS and HAF; 5--95\% width
$0.031$ at 2100); the weighting-ensemble width (500 flat-Dirichlet weight vectors
over the six carried variables; 5--95\% width $0.072$, a collinearity-limited
sensitivity band); and the across-scenario range over the four deterministic SSPs
($0.52$). The last dominates, identifying the emissions pathway as the leading
control on projected habitability.

\paragraph*{Validation.}
Skill is assessed on data not used in calibration. Over the withheld 1981--2020
window the emulator attains a GMST RMSE of $0.21$\,K with positive skill against
persistence ($+0.43$) and linear-trend ($+0.58$) baselines; over the cleaner
1981--2004 sub-window (before the ocean-heat constraint opens) the emulator RMSE is
$0.175$\,K versus $0.185$\,K (persistence) and $0.336$\,K (linear trend). The
posterior-mean ocean-heat trajectory matches the 2005--2020 observations to
$8$\,ZJ (Fig.~S1). These are genuine extrapolation tests. Basin-scale ocean,
freshwater and cryosphere comparisons require fields the two-parameter emulator
does not yet produce and are targets for the upgraded core.

\paragraph*{Data.}
All inputs are public and cached on first use: HadCRUT5 GMST\citep{Morice2021},
NOAA/NCEI 0--2000\,m OHC\citep{Levitus2012}, IPCC~AR6 ERF\citep{Forster2021}
extended along the SSPs\citep{Meinshausen2020, oneill2017roads}, the CMIP6/
Meinshausen CO$_2$ pathway, and the Natural Earth land mask. The gridded WHI and
land mask ship with the code as redistributable derivatives.
